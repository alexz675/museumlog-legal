# MuseumLog Community Guidelines

**Last updated:** July 27, 2026

MuseumLog has two places where what you write is visible to other people:
**community reviews** on artwork pages, and the **feedback board**. Your private
notes, your logs, and any review you keep private are never shown to anyone
else.

These guidelines say what belongs in those two places. They sit alongside the
[Terms of Service](terms); where the two overlap, the Terms
control.

---

## Write about the art

This is an app for people who look at art and want to say something about it.
Be specific, be opinionated, be wrong in interesting ways. A review that says
what you actually noticed is worth more than a polite one.

**Strong criticism is welcome.** "Overrated, badly hung, and the varnish has
gone yellow" is a legitimate review. Disliking an artist's work — loudly — is
not a violation of anything.

---

## Difficult subjects are not off-limits

Art is about nudity, violence, religion, race, sexuality, politics, death, war,
and colonialism. Serious writing about those subjects is exactly what this app
is for, and it will not be removed for being uncomfortable.

A review of Goya's *Disasters of War* that describes atrocity, a discussion of
how a museum acquired a looted object, a frank note about the eroticism of a
painting, an argument about a religious work — all of these belong here.

**The line is conduct, not subject matter.** We care about how you treat
*people*, not what the artwork depicts.

---

## What is not allowed

Content that:

- **harasses, demeans, or targets a person** — another user, an artist, a
  museum employee, anyone;
- **attacks people for who they are** — race, ethnicity, national origin,
  religion, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, age, disability, or any
  other protected characteristic;
- **threatens violence**, or encourages anyone to harm or kill themselves;
- **directs unwanted sexual attention** at a person;
- **exposes someone's private information** — address, phone number, email,
  workplace, or identity documents, whether yours or anyone else's;
- **is spam or a scam** — advertising, solicitation, or content unrelated to
  the artwork;
- **links to harmful destinations** — phishing, malware, or deceptive sites;
- **is part of a coordinated campaign** to brigade a user or manipulate ratings.

There is **no tolerance** for any of this.

## What happens when you publish

Most reviews publish immediately. Some are checked first.

| You see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Your review is now public.** | Published. |
| **Your review was saved and is being checked.** | Held briefly for review. It is saved and it is yours; it is just not visible to others yet. |
| **This review cannot be published in its current form.** | Not published. Your draft is intact — edit it and try again. |

Nothing is ever deleted because of a moderation decision. Content that cannot
be published stays in your account, editable.

We aim to act on reports within **24 hours**.

---

## Reporting and blocking

**Report** anything that breaks these guidelines. Reporting hides that item for
you straight away and sends it to us. The person you reported is never told who
reported them.

**Block** anyone you would rather not see. Blocking hides all of that person's
content from you — reviews *and* feedback posts — everywhere in the app. They
are not notified.

Reporting someone does not remove their content on its own. A person reviews it.

---

## If we get it wrong

We would rather hear about it than not.

- Content held or refused in error: email **museumlog.app@gmail.com** and we
  will look at it manually.
- Something we should have removed and did not: report it, or email us.

Repeatedly breaking these guidelines can lead to losing access to the community
features. That decision is always made by a person, never automatically, and
your account, logs, notes and collections are unaffected.
